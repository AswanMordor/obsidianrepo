## Learning
- def. **Behavioral Learning**: learns what behaviors are culturally appropriate and the consequences of specific behaviors
- def. **associative leanring/conditioning**: leanring associations between stimuli and responses

### Classical Conditioning
- def. **unconditioned stimulus**: "natural" stimuli
- def. **unconditioned response**: "natural" response to unconditioned stimulus
- def. **neutral stimulus**: a stimulus that elicits no response
- def. **conditioned**: associated with a unconditioned stimulus/response to elcict the same behavior
- where the period of associating a new conditioned response to a stimulus is called **aquisition**
- def. **spontaneous recovery**: the reemergence of a conditioned response after it was lessened 
- if the stimulus keeps coming without the porper association, however, the conditioned response will fade: **extinction**
- def. **stimulus generalization**: shows that subjects may response to similiar stimuli in addition to the original 
- however, **stimulus discrimination** could occur when the subject is trained to differentiate similiar stimuli by the presence or absence of the association 

### Operant Conditioning 
- where an individual becomes more/less likely to behave in a certain way depending on the consequences 
- where **reinforcements** increase the likeleyhood of the behavior, while **punsihments** decrease it
- def. **positive reinforcement**: the addition of a reinforcing stimulus
- def. **negative reinforcement**: the removal of an unpleasent stimulus
- def. **positive punishment**: the addition of a punishment
- def. **negative punishment**: the removal of a reinforcing stimulus
- **primary reinforcers/punishers** deal with psyiological needs ex. food, temp. homeostasis, and do NOT require learning (since they are more about innate survival)
- **secondary/conditioned reinforcers**: are not directly about survival ex. grades, money
- def. **escape conditioning**: allows the subject to escape an unpleasent stimulus 
- where **avoidance conditioning** allows the subject to to avoid the unpleasent stimulus altogether by a conditioned response to an association 
- def. **reinforcement schedule**: how often/what conditions a behavior is reinforced
- where **intervals** deal with time, and **ratios** deal with number of trials/attempts
- multiple steps of operant conditioning can be used to **shape** complex beahviors/responses
- def. **innate behvaiors**: developmentally fixed

### Observational Learning
- witnessing behavior, and modeling one's own behavior to be similar using **mirror neurons**, and **vicarious emotions**

Note: Classical Conditioning is associating a new stimulus with an *already learned or innate response (that has an unconditioned stimulus)* e.g. classical conditioning is assocaiting something new with an old behavior, while Operant Conditioning is creating a whole new behavior. 

## Behavior in a Biological Context
- has neural component, but NOT neccisarily emotional
- def. **behavior**: the sum of coordinated reponses to internal and external stimuli
- def. **animal signals**: vocalizations (not considered verbal comms because verbal comms need language), visual stimuli, touch, smell, etc...
- def. **social behavior**: interactions between members of the same species
	- **attraction**: drawing members of the species together
	- **aggression**: conflict and competition
	- **social support**: finding help through social connections
	- **foraging behavior** (animals): can be social as teamwork is oftern needed to decrease energy expenditure when obtaining food
	- **mating behavior**: are behaviors assocaited with reprodiction vis **mate choice**
	- **altruism**: disadvantageous to the individua, but bring benefits to the group; could the explanined by **inclusive fitness**, where the advantage of passing genes is not just for the individual, but for their relatives as well
- def. **game theory**: uses mathimatical models to represent complex multiplayer desicion-making...MUST have people involved

## Behavior in a Social Context
- **group** > social connection than **network**
- def. **organisation**: colelction of individuals working together towards a joint goal
- def. **self-presentation**: how indivduals present themselves to be percieved a certain way via **impresion management**
- def. **dramaturgical approach**: a form of impression management where behavior is always a performance where **front-stage-self** is shown to others and **back-stage-self** is jsut for those in the know, i.e. the individual

### Social Processes that Influence Behavior
- def. **groupthink**: group members tendency to think alike for the sake of group harmony, censoring ideas that go against the gorup norm, creating the issusion of unaniminity 
- def. **group polarization**: where the averaged sum of the groups desires is stronger (more extreme, i.e. more polarized) than any individual group member
- def. **social facilitation**: the tendency to perform better when an individual knows they are being watched by others (usually when doing talks they are comfortable with)
- def. **bystander effect**: where no one in a crowd will offer help because they assume someone else will...a type of diffusion of responsibility that increases with greater numbers of people 
- def. **social loafing**: members of the group work less, so others in the group work harder, e.g. freeloading
- def. **deindividualization**: the loss of individual awareness due to crowd participation; can cause actions/beahviors that would normally go against an individual's morality

## Behavior in a Cultural Context
- Culture helps individuals determine which behairos are appropriate in what contexts

### Socialization
- the process by which individuals learn the customs and values of thier culture, via operant conditioning
- def. **social norms**: rules (usually unwritten) that members of the community are expected to follow 
- def. **agents of socialization**: people/groups that influence personal attitudes, beliefs, or behaviors
- **conformity** dealts with the social norms of a group, while **obedience** is the behavioral change commanded by an authroity figure
- def. **deviance**: is when an individual does not recognize/follow social norms , often associated with a **stigma**: a negative label 

### Culture
- def. **assimiliation**: the process by which an individual or goup becomes par of a new culture 
- def. **subculture**: must be part of a larger culture, but with a small subset of differing attributes
