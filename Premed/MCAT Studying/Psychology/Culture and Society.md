## Culture

### Foundational Definitions
- def. **culture**: the beliefs, assumptions, objects, processes that make up a shared way of life
- def. **material culture**: object-focused, think waht an archeologist can uncover physically
- def. **non-material culture**: not physical culture, e.g., shared ideas, knowledge, beliefs etc. like religions
- def. **social norms**: govern what behavior is acceptable within a group
- def. **social group**: subset of population maintaining social interactions

### Evolution of Culture
* def. **symbolic culture**: elements of culture that have meaning only in the mind, where **beliefs** are in the form of symbols and their meanings are **values**
* some of the symbols used are **rituals**, certain accpeted seequences of behavior like a handshake

## Society, Systems, and Structures
def. **society**: 2+ individuals in community with a shared culture
def. **social institutions**: heirarchical systems to strucutre interpersonal interactions

### Common Social Instiutions
* **Goverment and Economy** provide a framework, rules, and order in society
* **Education** provides formal strucutre to childhood and devlopment, while transferring cultural knowledge
* **Religion** provides a system of how people interpret life and existence
* **Family** provides a system of child-bearing and raising
* **Health and Medicine** provide a system of sturcutred healthcare, but varies between approaches depending on society

### Demographics
- def. **Demographics**: stats. used to examine quantiative subsets of populations in order to describe that population including **age, gender, race, ethnicity, sexual orientation, immigration status**
- def. **demographic transition**: change in demographics over time
- def. **social movement**: a group of people with a shared idology who work together towards a specified set of goals
- def. **globalization**: the increasing amount of interaction and integration on an international scale

## Social Inequaltiy

### Foundational Definitions
- def. **spatial inequality**: the unequal distribution of resources and quality-of-life with a popualtion or geographic region
- def. **global inequalities**: disparities between regions/nations regarding GDP, natrual resources, employment oppotunities, access to healthcare
- def. **environmental justice**: inequalites regarding envionmental and health hazards (effected by region and the percieved value of the poeple living there)
- def. **residential segregation**: social inequality on the local scale, often affected by race and income level: defines education, healtcare, and service access

### Social Class
- def. **social class**: grouping members of society according to similarities in social standing; often heirarchical by nature
- def. **power**: influence over a community 
- def. **presitige**: the relative value of something within a class
- classes can have **upwards mobility** and **downwards mobility**, while castes cannot and are inherited
- **intergenerational mobility** is betwen generations, while **intragenerational mobility** is within a single generation
- **meritocracies** are built solely on merit
- **cultural captial** is about the social features that contibute to social mobility, while **social capital** is about who you know i.e. social connections
- **poverty**, the insufficeincy of monetary wealth or resources, can lead to **social exclusion**...where **absolute poverty** refers to the lact of essiantial resources: food, shelter, clothing, and hygiene

### Disparities in Health and Healthcare
- def. **health disparity**: differences in health and healthcare occuring between different groups of poeple: age, ethnicity, sex/gender, socioeconomic status/class, region
